# Effect and trim interactions

Frame treats the entire output timeline as one track, including split and reordered media. The Effects browser stays in place: single-click selects a catalogue item; double-click, Enter, + or dropping onto the timeline adds it. Each new effect owns its settings and a separate row. Clip selection controls trimming, ordering and clip looping.

Volume, equalizer, noise reduction, color/LUTs and audio/video fades support multiple independent instances. Drag an effect body to move its output range; drag either edge to resize it across any splits. The first correction covers the track, and later corrections start at the playhead. The first fade begins at the track start or finishes at its end; later fades start at the playhead. A fade only affects its own interval. Crop, transforms, speed and loudness normalization remain singleton track adjustments; their catalogue action becomes a check that reopens settings. Silence detection uses the source, and Music & voiceover provides one mixer with independently timed layers.

Clicking a bar opens a modeless settings overlay filling the visible right island. The heading identifies the effect and track. Changes apply immediately; Reset keeps the instance and its range, Remove deletes it, and undo restores it. The overlay follows panel resizing, has a scrollable body and fixed footer, fits phones and respects reduced motion. Playback and playhead scrubbing keep it open. Done, ×, Escape or clicking elsewhere closes it; keyboard dismissal returns focus to its bar. Legacy per-clip effects retain their editing controls when loaded.

Captions and watermarks begin at the playhead and last up to two output seconds. Each new caption or watermark has one bar even across a split. They remain at their output positions when media is reordered. Captions own their text, placement, export mode, font, size and colors. Preview objects open settings on click, support direct text editing on double-click, and resize using corner handles. Subtitle imports append individual captions; find/replace and output SRT export remain in Caption tools. Existing source-timed captions and watermarks continue to follow source content.

Effect bars have no close button. Right-click anywhere on a bar, including its edges, to delete that instance in one undoable action. Delete/Backspace and the settings overlay’s Remove button provide keyboard and touch alternatives; disabled timelines reject removal. Reset preserves a neutral effect’s bar and settings access. Clear track adjustments preserves captions, watermarks, music and media trimming in one undoable action. Recipes and copy/paste capture track adjustments and adapt crops and ranges to the receiving source.

Splitting never creates a second track effect. Older clip fades move into a track instance at their existing output interval before a split, preserving the ramp across both children. New effects use output timing for preview, processed frames, export, merge, project files and recovery. When the output is shortened, their ranges fit inside its remaining duration. General parameter keyframes and rearranging processing order are not yet supported.

The timeline uses sequence time and closes the leading gap after trimming. It keeps its scale while trimming; Fit frames the current sequence. Its axis expands when restored or slower footage needs more room. Adjacent clip handles stay inside their clips, and short clips/effects retain separate accessible handles with a minimum visible footprint. A 0.05-second output minimum protects valid sections. Restore footage using the edge that was trimmed; source bounds clamp quietly. Snapping includes split boundaries and other effect ranges, excluding the dragged item’s own edges.

Playback transport, time navigation and Space keep the effect overlay open. Space in text fields types normally; focused buttons, checkboxes and sliders route Space to playback. Other outside clicks, Done, × and Escape dismiss it. Desktop installed fonts are listed from the standard system and user font folders. Native exports copy the chosen family’s files into their temporary font directory. Missing fonts require choosing another font, and browser exports offer bundled fonts. Burned captions share one ASS resource/filter while retaining per-cue styles, so imported subtitle files do not create hundreds of rendering filters.

Clicking caption or watermark objects in the preview opens their settings without scrolling away from the preview. Double-click text or press F2 to edit it in place; Enter commits, Shift+Enter inserts a line break, and Escape cancels. Corner handles resize proportionally and preserve the opposite corner. Move/resize gestures commit one undo step and roll back on cancellation. Captions have a single editable Font combobox with filtered bundled/system choices and a full-width reset-position action.

Caption bodies drag the entire cue while edges trim its boundaries, including cues crossing split clips. Beginning an effect gesture opens or retains its overlay without stealing focus or scrolling the timeline. Short effect bars have a 48 px minimum hit area (64 px on phones) and separate edge handles; captions allocate rows using that visible footprint. Timing stays exact despite the enlarged target. Marker controls are removed; the mini timeline, horizontal scrolling and Locate playhead remain.

Transient notices use one reusable five-second component with a shrinking background countdown. This includes effect additions, validation errors, import reports, analysis results and notices below Export. Identical repeated additions restart their countdown. Ongoing job progress and browser Save file links remain available. Desktop OS notifications request closing after five seconds; their visual presentation is controlled by the OS.

For timed corrections, the first addition covers the track and later instances start at the playhead for up to two seconds. Body dragging moves the interval and edge dragging resizes it; keyboard arrows and boundary keys provide precise changes. Beginning any gesture opens its settings overlay and keeps it open. New intervals use output time across splits; legacy source intervals keep their cuts, reordering and speed mapping. Settings, timing and legacy overrides survive undo, autosave and project saves. Missing ranges in legacy projects retain their original whole-clip behavior.

Contiguous splits play through using the same media decoder and unchanged source clock. Clip identity advances without assigning `currentTime`, including when an animation frame crosses several short splits. Actual gaps and reordered clips still seek, and looping uses the existing playback boundary handling.

The playhead follows the draft position while an effect, caption or watermark bar moves; resizing follows the dragged edge. Mouse release commits one undo step. Escape cancels the gesture and restores both the bar and its original cursor position. Keyboard movement also updates the playhead. Music/voiceover layer movement uses the same output clock.

Every catalogue effect is available through Ctrl/Cmd+K. Commands explain media requirements, add independent repeatable instances, or open existing singleton settings. Repeatable catalogue actions keep the + icon after use; only existing singletons show the check and Edit action.
