# Effect and trim interactions

Frame uses a stable Effects browser and timeline-driven settings. Single-click selects a catalogue item; double-click, Enter, or + adds it to the visible target. Dragging onto a timeline clip targets that clip. Source effects retain their source scope. A new effect reveals a timeline bar even before changing its settings. Crop, transforms, volume, speed, fades, cleanup, equalizer, color/LUTs, silence detection and the mixer have one adjustment per target. Adding again reopens that target’s existing settings without resetting values or creating another undo step. Captions and watermarks create independently editable timed instances. The timeline expands its effect lanes and returns to All tracks when an additional effect appears.

Clicking an effect bar opens one settings overlay filling the visible height and width of the right island. It shows the effect, source name, and clip/source scope. For split clips, Apply settings to all clips switches between the clicked clip and shared adjustments; a note explains when clips have different values. Fades instead offer Apply to whole sequence, since shared fades run once at the output’s edges. The preview and timeline remain available, and changes apply immediately. Done, ×, Escape, or an outside click closes it; keyboard dismissal returns focus to the originating bar. The overlay follows panel resizing, has a scrollable body and fixed action footer, fits phones, and respects reduced motion. Audio cleanup overlays retain processed-audio preview and loudness analysis. Recipes, copy/paste, and Remove all clip effects live in the Effects browser.

Reset restores only the selected instance’s defaults and keeps its bar. Remove deletes only that instance. Added neutral effects survive undo, project files and autosave without changing export filters. Volume, speed and transforms follow the same interaction as other effects. A fade reset to zero has a full-clip settings bar so it can be edited again; active fades retain duration handles. Existing projects with active settings also get bars. Neutral overrides in older projects do not generate bars unless explicitly added.

Each effect bar offers an × action and Delete/Backspace removes a focused bar. Undo restores it. Captions and music tracks retain their direct removal affordance; clicking a caption cue also opens caption settings. Narrow bars hide × to avoid covering neighbours; zoom, Fit, keyboard removal and the overlay remain available. Source effects are labeled separately from clip effects. Remove all clip effects uses one undo step and preserves trimming, source captions, watermarks and music.

Captions and watermarks retain their own settings, identifiers and timeline bars. Each begins at the playhead and initially lasts up to two seconds; drag the body to move it or the edges to change its duration. Watermark text and artwork use the same range and follow trims, cut order and speed in preview, exports and merges. Untimed overlays from old projects still cover the entire edit. The first instance uses the original recipe fields for compatibility; validated `effectInstances` store additional parameters. Older projects with stacked corrections remain editable and export without discarding those parameters, but the catalogue no longer creates new correction stacks.

Each caption has its own text, placement and export mode with no shared source bar. Import, find/replace and SRT export live under Caption tools in the browser. Imports append individual effects, and older grouped captions are upgraded when loaded. Text/background colors, opacity, font, size, weight, italic, alignment and outline belong to each caption. Music & voiceover uses one mixer with multiple independently timed tracks; clicking or manipulating a track opens mixer settings. Final mix protection and ducking are output settings. General parameter keyframes and rearranging processing order are not yet supported.

The timeline retains its scale while trimming; Fit explicitly frames the current sequence. Its axis expands when restored or slower footage needs more room. Adjacent trim handles stay inside their own clips, so either side of a cut can be grabbed without hitting its neighbour. Main clips and audio excerpts use the same minimum visible footprint and separate handles as timed effects, so very short ranges remain expandable. A 0.05-second minimum preserves valid sections; floating-point tolerance keeps exact decimal boundaries valid for autosave and export. Faster clips need enough source footage to preserve that output minimum. Trimming and restoring outer edges remain available after shortening a split sequence. Restore footage using the edge that was trimmed; an edge at the original source boundary cannot invent additional frames, and an explanatory notice appears when dragged beyond that limit. Snap targets exclude the manipulated item’s own start and end so small outward drags can expand it.

Design references reviewed:

- [Premiere: apply effects](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/apply-video-effects/apply-effects.html): select a clip, drag an effect onto it, then adjust it in Effect Controls.
- [Premiere: find and group effects](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/apply-video-effects/find-and-group-effects.html): stable searchable category bins.
- [Premiere: Effect Controls panel](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/apply-video-effects/about-effect-controls-panel.html): selected-clip controls with expandable effect sections.
- [Premiere: remove effects](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/apply-video-effects/remove-effects-from-a-clip.html): selected effect removal, keyboard Delete, and removing attributes. Premiere retains fixed controls such as Volume; Frame resets their values and removes their timeline indicators.
- [Final Cut Pro: remove audio effects](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/remove-audio-effects-ver4d40b9cd6/mac): inspector-based effect removal and resetting built-in attributes to their defaults.

Playback transport, time navigation and Space keep the effect overlay open. Space in text fields types normally; focused buttons, checkboxes and sliders route Space to playback. Other outside clicks, Done, × and Escape dismiss it. Desktop installed fonts are listed from the standard system and user font folders. Native exports copy the chosen family’s files into their temporary font directory. Missing fonts require choosing another font, and browser exports offer bundled fonts. Burned captions share one ASS resource/filter while retaining per-cue styles, so imported subtitle files do not create hundreds of rendering filters.

Clicking caption or watermark objects in the preview opens their settings without scrolling away from the preview. Double-click text or press F2 to edit it in place; Enter commits, Shift+Enter inserts a line break, and Escape cancels. Corner handles resize proportionally and preserve the opposite corner. Move/resize gestures commit one undo step and roll back on cancellation. Captions have a single editable Font combobox with filtered bundled/system choices and a full-width reset-position action.

Caption bodies drag the entire cue while edges trim its boundaries, including cues crossing split clips. Beginning an effect gesture opens or retains its overlay without stealing focus or scrolling the timeline. Short effect bars have a 48 px minimum hit area (64 px on phones) and separate edge handles; captions allocate rows using that visible footprint. Timing stays exact despite the enlarged target. Marker controls are removed; the mini timeline, horizontal scrolling and Locate playhead remain.

Transient notices use one reusable five-second component with a shrinking background countdown. This includes effect additions, validation errors, import reports, analysis results and notices below Export. Identical repeated additions restart their countdown. Ongoing job progress and browser Save file links remain available. Desktop OS notifications request closing after five seconds; their visual presentation is controlled by the OS.
